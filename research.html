The Thesis

Spiking Vision-
Language-Action

Autonomous vehicles must perceive, understand and act in milliseconds and milliwatts. My PhD at AImotion Bavaria develops a vision-language-action (VLA) pipeline built entirely from spiking neural networks: an event-driven visual encoder, a vision-language layer that grounds what the car sees in language and reasoning, and an action layer that turns understanding into safe decisions — all designed to run on Intel Loihi neuromorphic hardware for energy-efficient, real-time deployment.

The Building Block

Anatomy of a
Spiking Neuron

Dendrites collect inputs (EPSPs) into the soma, which sums them over space and time. Past roughly −55 mV, the axon hillock fires an all-or-nothing action potential down the myelinated axon to the synaptic terminals, then resets. Stack billions of these and you get the brain — and the spiking networks I build to imitate it.

PhD Research Vision

The Next Frontier

Four building blocks, one goal — a complete spiking VLA agent for the road.

01 · Vision

Spiking Perception Module

An event-driven visual encoder for real-time object detection, segmentation and depth — turning camera and event-sensor streams into spike-based scene representations.

02 · Language

Vision-Language Alignment

Grounding spiking visual features in language for open-world understanding — spike-based vision-language pretraining and graph-spiking hybrid alignment.

04 · Hardware

Intel Loihi Deployment

Deploying the full perception–language–action loop on Loihi 2 neuromorphic silicon for orders-of-magnitude energy savings at the edge.

Neuromorphic, event-driven compute
Target Hardware

Intel Loihi 2

Conventional GPUs burn enormous power for driving perception. Intel's Loihi 2 takes a different path — programmable neuron models, on-chip learning, and asynchronous, event-driven computation. My work aims to bridge cutting-edge spiking VLA algorithms with this neuromorphic silicon for real-time perception and reasoning at a fraction of the power budget.

Driving Questions

Research Questions

The questions framing the thesis, tracked in my literature-review knowledge base.

01

Can a spiking VLA model perceive, reason and act for autonomous driving as well as conventional deep models?

Closing the accuracy gap between spike-based vision-language-action systems and dense ANNs for safety-critical perception and decision-making.

02

How do SNNs compare to CNNs in accuracy and energy efficiency for real-time object detection?

The foundational perception question — quantifying the accuracy / power trade-offs that make spiking perception viable on neuromorphic hardware.
