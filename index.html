Neurons communicating through spikes
The Idea

Compute With Spikes,
Not Numbers

Conventional deep networks process every pixel with dense arithmetic. Spiking neural networks (SNNs) instead communicate with sparse, asynchronous spikes — like biological neurons. Information lives in the timing of events, so computation happens only where and when something changes.

For autonomous driving — where milliwatts and milliseconds both matter — that promises detection that is faster at the edge and dramatically more energy-efficient than CNNs.

My thesis focus
The VLA Pipeline

What I Work On

My thesis builds a spiking vision-language-action model end to end — from event-driven perception, through vision-language reasoning, to safe action — all deployable on neuromorphic hardware.

01 · Vision

Spiking Perception

Event-driven detection, segmentation and depth as the visual encoder — built on SNN detectors like Spiking-YOLO, SpikingYOLOX and spiking transformers.

02 · Language

Vision-Language Alignment

Binding spiking visual features with language for open-world understanding and reasoning — spike-based vision-language pretraining and hybrid graph-spiking alignment.

03 · Action

Reasoning & Action

Turning perception and language into safe driving decisions — a spike-based reasoning and action layer for autonomous systems.

04 · Hardware

Neuromorphic Deployment

Running the full VLA pipeline on Intel Loihi for orders-of-magnitude lower energy at the edge.

Highlight From the Review

An SNN That Beats
the ANNs

On the GEN1 event-camera benchmark, SpikeDet reaches 47.6% AP at just 9.9 mJ — surpassing ANN detectors like RVT (47.2% AP, 116.7 mJ) while using an order of magnitude less power. On COCO it hits 52.2% AP at 40.4 mJ, rivalling YOLOv12-M at 3.8× the efficiency.

The key insight: fixing local firing saturation improves accuracy and energy at the same time.
